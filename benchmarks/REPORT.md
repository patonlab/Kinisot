## Baeyer-Villiger oxidation of cyclohexanone with m-CPBA: intermolecular KIEs

D. A. Singleton, M. J. Szymanski, J. Am. Chem. Soc. 1999, 121, 9455-9456 (doi:[10.1021/ja992016z](https://doi.org/10.1021/ja992016z)); 13C and 2H KIEs by NMR at natural abundance on recovered cyclohexanone, two reactions in CH2Cl2 taken to 79 ± 3% and 63 ± 3% conversion; values from Figure 1a. J. D. Crow, W. J. Hirschi, C. D. Clinton, J. S. Hirschi, ChemRxiv 2026 (preprint, not peer reviewed) (doi:[10.26434/chemrxiv.15002341/v1](https://doi.org/10.26434/chemrxiv.15002341/v1)); 13C KIEs on recovered cyclohexanone from 14 reactions at 14-80% conversion (600 MHz), weighted fit of R/R0 against calibrated conversion, averaged over three conversion methods; values and 95% confidence intervals from SI Tables S3a and S3b. Not computed yet.

Addition of the peracid to the carbonyl is rate limiting, so these KIEs probe that step; the alkyl migration comes after it (see baeyer_villiger_migration). All 13C KIEs are relative to the beta carbon (C3/C5), which both papers take as 1.000 (Figure 1a of the 1999 paper, whose text calls the C4 methylene group the internal standard; the 2026 text names C3); the 2H KIEs are relative to the C4 hydrogens. The 1999 rows list the two reactions' KIEs with their standard deviations, as in the paper, and are compared through their mean. The 2026 preprint's Scheme 1c prints 1.008(1) for the alpha carbon, which matches its fit with uncalibrated conversions (1.0087, SI Table S3d); the calibrated value the authors recommend is used here. The 2026 reactions ran at 0 C for 5 h and then at room temperature overnight (SI); the 1999 communication does not state the temperature. 298.15 K is used. Structures are still needed; the proton transfers accompanying the addition are not defined by the experiment (ref 12 of the 1999 paper). Rzepa computed omegaB97XD/Def2-TZVPP/SCRF=DCM models (blog, 2015, https://www.ch.ic.ac.uk/rzepa/blog/?p=14112) and deposited them with their force constants under data DOIs, but those files are no longer available (September 2026), so the structures have to be computed afresh. The DOIs were: complexed reactants (cyclohexanone, m-CPBA and m-chlorobenzoic acid as proton shuttle) 10.14469/ch/191315, acid-assisted addition transition structure TS1c 10.14469/ch/191312, and the separate reactants 10.14469/ch/191322, 191323 and 191324. He reports a C1 13C KIE of 1.023 for TS1c from the complexed reactants (1.021 from the separate ones), about half the measured value. For this intermolecular KIE, measured on recovered ketone, the reactant for the calculation is what is actually in the flask (Singleton, comment on the same post); he suspected the 1999 reactions used 60% m-CPBA under concentrated, heterogeneous conditions.

One frequency calculation serves as both reactant and product: the reactant carries CD3 on the axial methyl group (hydrogens 28-30 in this file), the product on the equatorial one (24-26). The EQE, (s/s')f_R / (s/s')f_P, is then [CD3 axial]/[CD3 equatorial]: CD3 slightly prefers the more crowded axial position. examples/eqe_cyclohexane computes the same equilibrium at B3LYP/6-31G(d).
